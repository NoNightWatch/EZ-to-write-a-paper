# Say one clear thing

[中文](one-clear-thing.md)

For now, try one small action: say something from your materials in everyday language. A few words, Chinese, English, or speech transcribed into text are all welcome. The sentence can be unfinished.

> What is one thing you already know from the material you have?

If you have already written a sentence, send it without rewriting it. If you only have a topic, say what you want to understand; we can start with a question. If you have no materials, ask for a fictional example.

“Material” can mean a quoted sentence, two versions of an instruction, a record, or notes you already have. You can send it before knowing whether it supports a whole paper. If the question above feels too broad, say: “I cannot see what to notice. Point to something in this passage and ask me one specific question.”

The assistant will identify what your sentence already communicates and help with one point that affects understanding. Clear wording can stay unchanged. Unconfirmed numbers, units, and sources will be identified rather than guessed.

An “I think…” statement can be a starting note. The assistant will explain whether it records your impression, asks a question, or describes something supported by the material, so a note is not mistaken for a paper's conclusion. You can keep answering in your own words; you do not have to fit a sentence frame.

You can send:

> Help me start with “Say one clear thing.” Do not show the whole course yet. Ask only the current question. Let me answer in a few words or ordinary language, then help me form a sentence. I will tell you if I want a demonstration or a pause.

<summary>Would you like a small example first?</summary>

All of this example is fictional teaching material: Table 1 records a mean completion time of 8 minutes for group A and 11 minutes for group B.

You say: “Group A took 8 minutes on average, and group B took 11 minutes.”

Assistant: “This sentence identifies the groups, mean completion time, and values. It can serve as a sentence in a results section. With the supplied material, you can add ‘see Table 1’. You now have a description that can be traced to its record.”

If no table or other context has been supplied and you only say “A is 8 and B is 11,” the assistant asks what the numbers represent; it does not add means or minutes on its own. You need not repeat information already provided. Real writing still requires checking the actual records.

</details>

A rough draft can be repetitive, wordy, or out of order. Wording can be revised gradually; facts and sources need checking. Finishing one sentence is a valid stopping point. You do not need to consider journals, sections, and polished English at the same time.

If you want to continue, add a related observation or identify its source. You can also request direct editing, a demonstration, or the [complete-paper course](first-paper-course.en.md). This entry is optional, with no grades, countdowns, or compulsory task list.
