# Your first paper: learner workbook

[中文练习册](paper-workbook.zh-CN.md) · [Course entry](first-paper-course.en.md) · [Evidence packet](paper-case-materials.md)

All exercises use fictional teaching materials. You may answer in notes, Chinese, or English. Try first if useful, then open the answer link at the end; you may also request a demonstration immediately. Suggested lengths keep exercises manageable and are not journal requirements. There is no single correct wording.

<a id="E01"></a>
## E01 · Identify what is available

Read P01/P02. Write four lines: the objects studied; the numbers of documents and pairs; two directly recordable features; and two unmeasured outcomes.

What evidence would each claim require: “The later version is easier to understand,” “Later numbered items can be counted,” and “A contact line guarantees help”? Cite the relevant P record when explaining your decision.

Hint: locate `twelve documents` and the unmeasured outcomes in P01. Documents, pairs, and readers are different units.

Check: a reader can identify what you have and which claims need further evidence.

<a id="E02"></a>
## E02 · Write an answerable question

Replace “How can the learning centre become more efficient?” with one question the packet can answer. Then write one sentence about the purpose of each section: introduction, methods, results, and discussion. You do not need to draft four sections yet.

Hint: name the objects, two versions, and recorded features. Do not add reader outcomes.

Check: each main term in the question can be traced to P01/P02, and each section has a task related to that question.

<a id="E03"></a>
## E03 · Make the method checkable

Count C01-B and explain why its second item counts once. Write roughly 100 to 160 English words, or a corresponding Chinese paragraph, covering material selection, the unit, both definitions, and the summaries. Do not add coding or testing procedures that were never performed.

Hint: first explain “what I received, how I counted, and how I summarized” in ordinary language.

Check: another reader can use your description to obtain the same C01-B counts.

Calculate both totals, the means per card, and relative change using the earlier total as the denominator. Write three to five result sentences, including a paired pattern and contact counts. Identify and repair unsupported parts of this sentence:

<a id="E05"></a>
## E05 · Introduce the question

Read the complete L1/L2 cards. For each, state what it supports and what it leaves unestablished. Then write two or three short introduction paragraphs, roughly 150 to 220 English words or a corresponding Chinese text. Include the relationship between the cards, the question, and the scope of this paper.

Remove or qualify unsupported claims such as `Many studies prove...` and `Nobody has studied...`. Place each citation beside the claim it supports. Do not invent journals, dates, or DOIs for teaching notes.

Hint: explain the observable document features, then the particular comparison.

Check: your results can answer the question posed at the end of the introduction.

<a id="E06"></a>
## E06 · Develop discussion and conclusion

Read P04 and compare the original wording of both versions of C01/C06. Write three short discussion paragraphs: answer the question; explain the grouping changes supported by the text; identify reader or service conclusions that remain unestablished. Add a one- or two-sentence conclusion.

Annotate explanations as recorded result, material-supported interpretation, or needing further evidence. Keep these labels in working notes; remove them from a clean draft if appropriate.

Hint: `fewer numbered items` is directly supported; `fewer actions required` cannot be inferred from numbering alone.

Check: discussion develops the findings, interpretations have source locations, and the conclusion stays within the collection.

<a id="E07"></a>
## E07 · Title and abstract

Write an accurate title, three to five keywords, and an abstract of approximately 120 to 180 English words, or a corresponding Chinese abstract. Include the question, materials and method, main results, and scope. Trace every number to your results table. Retain the fictional-teaching disclosure.

Hint: select the essential information from the completed sections, then connect it into a paragraph.

Check: the abstract identifies the objects, method, findings, and limits without introducing a new claim.

<a id="E08"></a>
## E08 · Assemble a complete practice manuscript

Combine title, abstract, keywords, introduction, methods, results with table caption, discussion, conclusion, and references. Keep the fictional disclosure. Check six pairs/twelve documents, units, totals/means, contact counts, L1/L2 attribution, and scope across sections.

Ask for the two most useful revisions. Revise once yourself and compare versions. If no substantive problem is found, finish without forcing extra edits. Write a version note describing changes and unchecked items. You may consult the complete model paper, distinguishing a guided correction from your own judgment.

Check: you have a connected full draft, traceable evidence, and an honest account of unfinished checks. The fictional paper must not be submitted as research.

<a id="T01"></a>
## T01 · Try different materials

These fictional records concern four matched event notices, eight documents in total. You have completed count records, **not the original notice texts**. P02's counting definitions apply to the table. There are no reader or event-outcome data. Describe the records and acknowledge that the source text cannot be checked; do not claim to have recounted it. Version labels indicate sequence only. No sampling, inferential testing, or reliability assessment is documented.

| Notice | Earlier items | Later items | Earlier contact | Later contact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N01 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| N02 | 4 | 4 | 1 | 0 |
| N03 | 3 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| N04 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 1 |

Without reopening the model paper:

1. Write a question and two materials/methods sentences stating what you actually accessed.
2. Calculate totals, means, and paired changes; write a results paragraph including contact counts.
3. Write a discussion paragraph and a four-sentence abstract supported by the new records.
4. Explain which conclusion differs from the original case and which interpretation cannot be checked without the texts.

Submit an unassisted attempt first if you wish, then request hints or answers. Assistance is recorded accurately; viewing a model does not establish independent performance.

### Optional: transfer to other genres

These tasks concern expression and organization, not the invention of subject-specific theory.

- Historical texts: two fictional preface records say “for workshop apprentices” (1910) and “for classroom instruction” (1920). Write a comparison and a scope sentence. No student-use records or evidence of author motives are supplied.
- Mathematics: a supplied proposition applies only to “finite graphs with maximum degree at most 4.” No proof is supplied. Write an accurate result summary and list the material needed to organize a proof section. Do not broaden the conditions or invent a proof.
- Review: synthesize L1/L2 into one bounded paragraph. Without database-search records, do not label it a systematic review.
